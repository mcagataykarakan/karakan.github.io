About Me

I am a mechanical and biomedical engineering researcher with over a decade of interdisciplinary experience spanning MEMS/NEMS, microfabrication, organ-on-chip platforms, and bio-integrated device design. Currently, I am a Postdoctoral Researcher in Prof. Christopher S. Chen's lab at Boston University and the Wyss Institute at Harvard University.

My postdoctoral work centers on developing next-generation microphysiological systems for human cardiac tissue engineering — integrating electromechanical stimulation, two-photon direct laser writing, flexible electronics, and embedded force/electrophysiology sensing. I also supervise the Multiscale Laser Lithography Laboratory at BU, supporting research in MEMS, micro-robotics, optics, and tissue engineering.

My Ph.D. work produced the first direct-laser-written heart-on-a-chip platform with integrated transducers, published as a co-first-author paper in Lab on a Chip (2021) and followed by Science Advances (2022) and Lab on a Chip (2024). During my M.S. at Bilkent University / UNAM, I designed and fabricated multi-mode NEMS for volatile organic compound sensing and cancer biomarker detection.

Research Highlights

Cardiac Tissue Engineering
Direct Laser-Written Heart-on-a-Chip with Integrated Transducers

Developed the first two-photon direct laser-written (DLW) microfluidic heart-on-a-chip platform incorporating high-precision polymer scaffolds with integrated force and electrophysiology transducers. Enables generation, electromechanical stimulation, and real-time characterization of 3D engineered cardiac microbundles. Co-first author paper in Lab on a Chip.

Lab on a Chip (2021) · Co-First Author
Cardiac Tissue Engineering
Living Cardiac Pump on a Chip

Engineered a living cardiac pump using high-precision microfabrication and hiPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, demonstrating fluid actuation driven by tissue contractility. The platform integrates force sensing and electromechanical stimulation for functional assessment of engineered cardiac tissues. Published in Science Advances.

Science Advances (2022) · NSF CELL-MET ERC
Cardiac Tissue Engineering
Geometry & Length Control of 3D Engineered Heart Tissues via DLW

Demonstrated systematic geometry and length control of 3D engineered heart tissues using direct laser writing, enabling scalable and reproducible tissue formats for drug screening and maturation studies. Engineered porous metamaterial scaffolds that increased platform throughput 4× and enabled >1 month long-term culture.

Lab on a Chip (2024)
MEMS / NEMS
Functionalized NEMS for Cancer Biomarker Detection

Designed and fabricated multi-mode nanoelectromechanical systems with electrothermal actuation and piezoresistive readout at Bilkent/UNAM. Functionalized sensors via vacuum organic thin-film evaporation to detect volatile organic compound (VOC) biomarkers of colorectal cancer in isolation. Developed intermodal coupling techniques for enhanced mode detection.

IEEE Sensors Journal (2023) · Physical Review Applied (2018)
MEMS / NEMS
Frequency-Dependent Piezoresistive Effect in Gold Nanoresistors

Contributed to investigation of frequency-dependent piezoresistive effects in top-down fabricated gold nanoresistors, providing fundamental insights into nanoscale electromechanical coupling relevant to high-frequency NEMS sensor design and signal transduction at the nanoscale.

Nano Letters (2021)
MEMS / NEMS
Polymer MEMS Resonators with Integrated Transducers via DLW

Developing direct laser-written polymeric MEMS resonators with on-chip integrated transducers, combining the design flexibility of two-photon DLW with precision electromechanical readout. Applications include mass sensing, force detection, and lab-on-a-chip systems with embedded mechanical components.

In Review (2026) · Multiscale Laser Lithography Lab, BU
Microfluidic Devices
Integrated Microfluidic Sensing Platforms

Designed and validated a suite of microfluidic devices with integrated impedance electrodes: a Coulter-principle flow monitoring sensor, a red blood cell trapping and membrane-fluctuation viability sensor, and a rapid antibiotic susceptibility testing (AST) device identifying effective treatment within one hour via bacterial motion measurement.

BU Graduate Research · Four-probe lock-in impedance methods
3D-Printed Anchors
Porous 3D-Printed Anchors for Suspended Tissue Engineering

Developing scalable porous anchors as mechanical interfaces for suspended tissue culture systems, fabricated via two-photon direct laser writing with tunable porosity and geometry. These anchors enable long-term tissue culture under tension, reliable tissue attachment, and modular/reconfigurable tissue formats for microphysiological applications.

Engineering in Cardiovascular Medicine (2026, poster)
